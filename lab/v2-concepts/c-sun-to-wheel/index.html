1500 miles on sunlight

Introducing Rice's first American Solar Challenge car—and the team needed to take it racing, both cross-country and on track.

Scroll to raise the sun and follow the energy from the array to the rear wheel.

Carrera, side elevation. 400 lbs estimated driverless weight, 40% / 60% front/rear weight distribution, aluminum suspension.

1Collect

1000 W solar array

Sunlight lands on the array that covers Carrera's top surface. Its cells convert that light into electricity while the car drives.

2Store

5.18 kWh battery (design)

The battery buffers energy between the array and the motor, so power is there when the car needs it. A silicon-anode battery pack is in development.

3Drive

Hub motor, rear wheel

The inverter controls a hub motor that drives a rear wheel, with 97.2% motor + inverter efficiency. Low rolling resistance tires keep losses down where the car meets the road.

X-ray view: the body turns transparent so you can follow the power to the wheel.

4Optimize

Go farther. Use less energy.

Telemetry and models choose an efficient pace. The driver dashboard and custom power boards keep the whole energy path visible to the team.

Rice's only student-built wheel-to-wheel racing team

Carrera is in development for Formula Sun Grand Prix 2027: three eight-hour race days on a closed course, and the qualifier for the American Solar Challenge.
